TI-84 Plus CE: the best TI-84 calculator for most students

If you want one calculator that will last from Algebra 2 to college, buy the TI-84 Plus CE. Released in 2015, it keeps the keyboard and menus of the classic TI-84 but adds a backlit colour screen of 320 × 240 pixels, which makes graphs, tables and statistics far easier to read. The rechargeable battery charges over USB, so you never hunt for AAA batteries the night before an exam. It is the model most teachers demonstrate on, which means the screens in your lessons match yours.

TI-84 Plus CE Python: worth it if you code

The Python edition does everything the standard CE does and adds the ability to write and run Python programs. For pure maths, graphing and statistics there is no difference. If you are taking computer science, robotics or simply enjoy programming, it is a fun upgrade; otherwise the standard CE is all you need.

TI-84 Plus and TI-83 Plus: the budget route

The original TI-84 Plus (2004) and the older TI-83 Plus (1999) have small black-and-white screens and run on four AAA batteries, but they handle the same algebra, graphing and statistics tasks your teacher will set. Because so many were sold, they are easy to find second-hand. The trade-off is a slower processor, less memory and a screen that is harder to read in poor light.

How to buy a used TI-84 safely

  • Test every key on the home screen, including the arrow pad and 2nd.
  • Check the screen for dead lines, faded areas or cracks.
  • Open the battery cover and look for corrosion on older AAA models.
  • View the About screen (2nd + → About) to confirm the exact model and OS version.
  • Reset it after buying to clear old programs and settings.
  • Be wary of prices that look too good: look-alike calculators exist, so buy from sellers with good reviews and returns.

Do you actually need a graphing calculator?

Not always. Middle school, Algebra 1, chemistry and many tests that ban graphing calculators only need a good scientific model. The TI-30XS calculator is the favourite here, with a four-line display that shows fractions and expressions the way they appear in your textbook. For younger students, the TI-34 calculator focuses on fractions and step-by-step maths at a lower price. You can always move up to a TI-84 when your course requires graphing.

Exams: what is allowed?

TI-84 Plus family calculators are accepted on the calculator sections of the SAT, the ACT and AP exams, which is a big reason schools standardise on them. Calculators with a computer algebra system are restricted on some tests, such as the ACT, so the non-CAS TI-84 is the safe choice. Policies change from year to year, so always read the official calculator policy before your test date.

When to buy

Prices on graphing calculators move around during the year, and back-to-school season in late summer is often when retailers run promotions. Check whether your school lends calculators before you buy, and ask your teacher which model they use in class; matching their screen makes lessons much easier to follow.

Our verdict

Buy the TI-84 Plus CE if you can; it is the best TI-84 calculator for almost every high-school and college student. Pick the CE Python if you want to code, a used TI-84 Plus if budget is the priority, and a TI-30XS or TI-34 if your course doesn't need graphing yet. Whatever you choose, practising the keystrokes before exam day will matter more than the model you own.

Best TI 84 calculator FAQ

What is the best TI-84 calculator to buy?

For most students the TI-84 Plus CE is the best TI-84 calculator: it has a backlit colour screen, a rechargeable battery and is accepted on the SAT, ACT and AP exams. Choose the TI-84 Plus CE Python if you also want to code.

Is the TI-84 Plus CE better than the TI-84 Plus?

For daily use, yes. The CE is thinner and lighter, has a backlit colour screen of 320 × 240 pixels and a USB-rechargeable battery. The TI-84 Plus has a 96 × 64 black-and-white screen and runs on AAA batteries, but the core maths functions are very similar.

What is the difference between the TI-84 Plus CE and the TI-84 Plus CE Python?

The Python edition can write and run Python programs on the calculator. For maths, graphing and statistics, the two work the same way.

Is a used TI-84 Plus worth buying?

Often, yes. Check that every key responds, the screen has no dead lines, the battery cover is present and the calculator resets cleanly. Ask the seller to show the About screen so you know the model and operating system.

Can I use a TI-84 on the SAT and ACT?

Yes. TI-84 Plus family models are on the accepted lists for the SAT, ACT and AP exams. Rules can change, so always check the official calculator policy for your test date.

Do I need a graphing calculator for algebra?

Not always. Many Algebra 1 and middle-school classes only need a scientific calculator such as the TI-30XS MultiView or TI-34 MultiView. Graphing calculators become important from Algebra 2 and pre-calculus onward.

Is the TI-83 Plus still good enough?

For many high-school classes, yes. Its menus are almost identical to the TI-84, though it has less memory, a slower processor and a basic screen.

Can I try a TI-84 before buying one?

Yes. You can practise with a free TI-84 calculator online to learn the keys and menus, then decide whether you need the handheld model.
